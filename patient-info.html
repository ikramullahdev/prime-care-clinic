Patient Information

Everything you need to know to prepare for your visit.

Before Your Visit

Preparing for Your Appointment

A little preparation helps ensure your consultation is thorough and productive.

📋

Medical Records

Bring any previous medical reports, lab results, diagnostic imaging, or discharge summaries from other facilities. These help the physician understand your medical history and avoid repeating tests unnecessarily.

💊

Current Medications

Make a list of all medications you are currently taking, including prescription drugs, over-the-counter medications, vitamins, and herbal supplements. Include the dosage and how often you take each one.

📝

Symptom Notes

Write down your symptoms before your visit. Note when they started, how often they occur, what makes them better or worse, and any patterns you have noticed. This ensures nothing important is forgotten during the consultation.

🪪

Identification

Bring a valid form of identification such as your CNIC (Computerized National Identity Card) or passport. This is needed for patient records and to ensure accurate identification.

At the Clinic

During Your Visit

Arrival

Please arrive a few minutes before your scheduled appointment to allow time for check-in. If this is your first visit, you may need to provide basic personal and medical history information.

The Consultation

The physician will review your medical history, discuss your current concerns, and perform a clinical examination as needed. Depending on the nature of your visit, the physician may:

  • Order diagnostic tests (blood work, imaging, etc.)
  • Prescribe medications
  • Recommend lifestyle changes
  • Refer you to a specialist if needed
  • Schedule a follow-up appointment

Ask Questions

Your visit is the ideal time to ask questions about your condition, treatment plan, or any concerns you may have. Do not hesitate to ask for clarification on anything you do not understand. Write your questions down beforehand so you do not forget them.

For Diabetes Patients

If you are visiting for diabetes care, please bring:

  • Your blood glucose monitoring log (if you keep one)
  • Your glucose meter (the doctor may want to check its accuracy)
  • Recent HbA1c results if available
  • A record of what you typically eat in a day (helpful for dietary counseling)
Follow-Up

After Your Visit

Follow Your Treatment Plan

Take prescribed medications as directed and follow any lifestyle recommendations provided. If you experience unexpected side effects or your symptoms change, contact the clinic rather than adjusting medications on your own.

Keep Your Follow-Up Appointments

If a follow-up visit was recommended, make sure to schedule and attend it. Follow-up appointments are particularly important for chronic conditions like diabetes, where regular monitoring directly affects treatment outcomes.

Lab Results & Reports

If tests were ordered, keep copies of all results for your records. The physician will review the results with you at your next appointment or contact you if immediate attention is needed.

When to Seek Urgent Help

This clinic provides outpatient consultations. If you experience a medical emergency — such as chest pain, difficulty breathing, loss of consciousness, severe allergic reaction, or dangerously low blood sugar — please go to the nearest emergency department or call emergency services immediately.
